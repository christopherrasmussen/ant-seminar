Abstracts

From Gauss composition to binary orthogonal modular forms [top]

Haochen Wu, Trinity College

Gauss composition identifies equivalence classes of binary quadratic forms with ideal classes in quadratic orders. We revisit it over Dedekind domains, showing that it is given by the Clifford and norm maps, building on work of Kneser and Wood. We clarify the role of orientations and the natural emergence of narrow class groups. As an application, we show that binary orthogonal eigenforms correspond to Hecke characters. Using theta series, we show the explicit connection between Hilbert modular forms and orthogonal modular forms arising from positive definite binary lattices over the ring of integers of a totally real number field. This is joint work with John Voight.

Heavenly elliptic curves over cubic fields [top]

Suzanne O'Hara, Wesleyan University

The study of heavenly elliptic curves is motivated by a question of Ihara which compares Galois extensions generated by arithmetic and geometric methods. Knowing an elliptic curve \(E/K\) is heavenly an odd prime \(\ell\) lets us deduce extra information about the representation of the absolute Galois group, \(G_K\), on the \(\ell\)-torsion of the curve. Using this, we deduce a formula for the mod \(\ell\) trace of \(E\) at primes \(p\) distinct from \(\ell\). Previous work of Rasmussen and Tamagawa has shown that for a fixed number field \(K\) of degree at most \(3\), there are on finitely many \(K\)-isomorphism classes of heavenly elliptic curves when we let \(\ell\) vary through all primes. Recently, McLeman and Rasmussen have studied the set of heavenly elliptic curves where both the quadratic field of definition and the prime \(\ell\) are allowed to vary. In this talk, we discuss analogues of their results to the cubic field case and show a further classification of heavenly elliptic curves which have a “CM-like” trace.

Sesquilinear pairings and isogeny-based cryptography [top]

Joe Macula, Wake Forest University

Bilinear pairings on elliptic curves, in particular the Weil and Tate pairings, have been used extensively in cryptography in general and in isogeny-based cryptography in particular. Following the break of the Supersingular Isogeny Diffie Helman (SIDH) protocol, it was shown that the ideas of this attack can be leveraged to attack certain instances of protocols based on the class group action problem, with pairings serving as a crucial link. In this talk, I'll discuss some of the background theory of pairings and introduce the sesquilinear pairings of Stange (2026), then give an overview of recent work in collaboration with Stange (2024) on further extending the attacks on class group action protocols. Along the way, we'll also discuss some of the relevant isogeny-based cryptography.

Elliptic curve rank growth in multi-quadratic fields [top]

Dylan Costa, University of Connecticut

Let \(E\) be an elliptic curve over \(\mathbb{Q}\). The rank of \(E\) over a number field \(K\) is the maximal number of \(K\)-rational points on \(E\) that are linearly independent over \(\mathbb{Z}\). A natural question is how the rank grows when we enlarge the base field from \(\mathbb{Q}\) to a number field \(K\). In this talk, we study the case where \(K\) is a multi-quadratic extension of \(\mathbb{Q}\). We give an explicit construction of square-free integers \(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}\) such that \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d_{1}}, \ldots,\sqrt{d_{n}})\) has degree \(2^{n}\). Each quadratic twist \(E^{d_{k}}\) has a rational point of infinite order and a global root number that we choose in advance. As a result, the rank of \(E\) over \(K\) is at least the rank of \(E\) over \(\mathbb{Q}\) plus \(n\), and the torsion subgroup of \(E\) does not grow from \(\mathbb{Q}\) to \(K\). If we further assume the Parity Conjecture, a good choice of root numbers makes the rank increase even more. The increase is at least \(\max \{2n + 2^{n-1} - 1,\, 2^{n}-1 \} \) when the global root number is \(-1\), and at least \(\max \{2n,\,2^{n-1}+2n-2\}\) when it is \(+1\). Lastly, we apply these ideas to an elliptic curve over \(\mathbb{Q}\) of rank at least \(32\). We find an explicit quadratic field over which this curve has rank at least \(34\) and root number \(-1\), and so rank at least \(35\) under the Parity Conjecture. This is joint work with Álvaro Lozano-Robledo.
