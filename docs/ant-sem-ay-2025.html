Abstracts

A Constructive and Stable Cartan-Dieudonné Theorem for Binary Quadratic Forms

Ze Fan, Wesleyan University

The classical Cartan-Dieudonné theorem states that every isometry of a non-degenerate quadratic form is a product of reflections. We present a fully explicit and quantitative version of this theorem for binary forms over non-archimedean local fields of characteristic zero. For any isometry, we provide an explicit decomposition into a product of two reflections. A key result is the stability of this factorization: we establish quantitative control on how the reflection matrices behave under small perturbations of the underlying vectors. We will conclude with applications to studying the equivalence of binary quadratic forms over number fields. This is joint work with Professor Han Li.

Counting primitive integral solutions to generalized Fermat equations

Santiago Arango-Piñeros, UMass Amherst

Let \(F\colon Ax^a + By^b + Cz^c = 0\) be a generalized Fermat equation with nonzero integer coefficients. A solution \((x,y,z) \in \mathbb{Z}^3\) is called primitive if \(\gcd(x,y,z) = 1\). We prove that when \(\chi = 1/a + 1/b + 1/c - 1 > 0\), the counting function \(N(F;h)\) of primitive integral solutions of height at most \(h\) satisfies \[ N(F;h) \sim k(F)\, h^{\chi} \] for some constant \(k(F) \geq 0\), as \(h\) goes to infinity. This result refines a theorem of Beukers, and the proof relies on the stack-theoretic perspective introduced by Poonen–Schaefer–Stoll in their study of \(x^2 + y^3 + z^7 = 0\).

Hypergeometric Decompositions of K3 Surface Pencils

Leah Sturman, Southern Connecticut State University

In this talk we will look at five pencils of projective quartic surfaces with the aim of giving explicit formulas for the point counts over finite fields of each. These point counts are written in terms of hypergeometric sums. Given time, we will discuss how to obtain a decomposition of the incomplete L-function of each pencil in terms of hypergeometric L-series and Dedekind zeta functions. This is joint work with Rachel Davis, Jessamyn Dukes, Thais Gomes Ribeiro, Eli Orvis, Adriana Salerno, and Ursula Whitcher.

Counting biquadratic number fields that admit quaternionic or dihedral extensions

Louis Gaudet, UMass Amherst

Many interesting problems in arithmetic statistics involve counting number fields (ordered by their discriminants, say) with certain properties. In joint work with Siman Wong (UMass Amherst), we establish asymptotic formulae for the number of biquadratic extensions of \(\mathbb{Q}\) that admit a degree-2 extension with Galois group \(G\), where \(G\) is either the quaternion group or the dihedral group (of order 8). We will discuss these results and how they are proved, and we will discuss their significance with regard to a theorem of Tate on lifts of projective Galois representations.

The distribution of 2-Selmer groups in quadratic twist families of elliptic curves

Ariel Weiss, Trinity College

The Poonen–Rains and Bhargava–Kane–Lenstra–Poonen–Rains heuristics give striking predictions for the distribution of Selmer groups in the family of all elliptic curves over \(\mathbb{Q}\). In particular, they predict that the average size of the \(p\)-Selmer group is \(1+p\), a result proved for \(p = 2\), \(3\), and \(5\) by Bhargava and Shankar.

However, these models do not accurately describe families of elliptic curves with isogenies, where the average \(p\)-Selmer size can even be infinite. In this talk, I will report on work in progress to determine the distribution of 2-Selmer groups in the family of quadratic twists of an elliptic curve with a 2-torsion point. I will present a theorem that shows that the distribution of the 2-Selmer groups coincides with a distribution arising from the kernels of random matrices. This work is joint with Harald Helfgott, Zev Klagsbrun, and Jennifer Park.

The group theory of elliptic curves in towers of finite fields

John Cullinan, Bard College

If \(E\) and \(E'\) are elliptic curves defined over a finite field \(k\) such that \(E(k)\) and \(E'(k)\) have the same order, what is the likelihood that they define isomorphic groups? In this talk we will address this question from two points of view: fix \(E\), vary \(p\), and fix \(p\), vary \(E\). We will not assume more than a working knowledge of elliptic curves. This is recent and ongoing work with Nathan Kaplan of UC Irvine.

The Uniformization Theorem

Will Allen, Wesleyan University (honors talk)

The Uniformization Theorem establishes, up to appropriate notions of equivalence, a one-to-one correspondence between lattices in the complex plane and complex elliptic curves. In this talk, we give an overview of the correspondence, and then explore in detail one of the properties of this correspondence: that for any lattice \(\Lambda\) which is associated to an elliptic curve \(E_\Lambda\), there is a bijection \(\mathbb{C}/\Lambda \to E_\Lambda\) that gives an isomorphism of both Riemann surfaces and abelian groups.

On the Cuspidal Cohomology of Congruence Subgroups of \(\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{Z})\)

Zachary Porat, Wesleyan University (PhD thesis defense)

In their 1984 work, Ash, Grayson, and Green compute the action of Hecke operators on the cohomology of congruence subgroups \(\Gamma_0(3,p) \leq \mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{Z})\) for small \(p\). A natural extension is to work with other congruence subgroups of \(\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{Z})\). In the first part of the talk, we detail techniques for working with congruence subgroups that are Iwahori at \(p\), computing the action of Hecke operators on the corresponding cohomology. In the second part of the talk, we return to the \(\Gamma_0(3,p)\) setting, introducing a new technique which allows for computations directly on the cuspidal cohomology.
