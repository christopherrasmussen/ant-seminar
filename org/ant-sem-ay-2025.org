*** Abstracts

***** /A constructive and stable Cartan-Dieudonné theorem for binary quadratic forms/
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: 2025-09-26
:END:
      [[https://zfan-math.com/][Ze Fan]], Wesleyan University

The classical Cartan-Dieudonné theorem states that every isometry of a
non-degenerate quadratic form is a product of reflections. We present a
fully explicit and quantitative version of this theorem for binary forms
over non-archimedean local fields of characteristic zero. For any
isometry, we provide an explicit decomposition into a product of two
reflections. A key result is the stability of this factorization: we
establish quantitative control on how the reflection matrices behave
under small perturbations of the underlying vectors. We will conclude
with applications to studying the equivalence of binary quadratic forms
over number fields. This is joint work with Professor Han Li.

***** /Counting primitive integral solutions to generalized Fermat equations/
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: 2025-11-07
:END:
      [[https://sarangop1728.github.io/][Santiago Arango-Piñeros]], UMass Amherst

Let \(F\colon Ax^a + By^b + Cz^c = 0\) be a generalized Fermat equation
with nonzero integer coefficients. A solution \((x,y,z) \in
\mathbb{Z}^3\) is called primitive if \(\gcd(x,y,z) = 1\). We prove that
when \(\chi = 1/a + 1/b + 1/c - 1 > 0\), the counting function
\(N(F;h)\) of primitive integral solutions of height at most \(h\)
satisfies
\[ N(F;h) \sim k(F)\, h^{\chi} \]
for some constant \(k(F) \geq 0\), as \(h\) goes to infinity. This
result refines a theorem of Beukers, and the proof relies on the
stack-theoretic perspective introduced by Poonen–Schaefer–Stoll in
their study of \(x^2 + y^3 + z^7 = 0\).

***** /Hypergeometric decompositions of K3 surface pencils/
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: 2025-11-14
:END:
      [[https://www.southernct.edu/directory/sturmanl1][Leah Sturman]], Southern Connecticut State University

In this talk we will look at five pencils of projective quartic surfaces
with the aim of giving explicit formulas for the point counts over
finite fields of each. These point counts are written in terms of
hypergeometric sums. Given time, we will discuss how to obtain a
decomposition of the incomplete L-function of each pencil in terms of
hypergeometric L-series and Dedekind zeta functions. This is joint work
with Rachel Davis, Jessamyn Dukes, Thais Gomes Ribeiro, Eli Orvis,
Adriana Salerno, and Ursula Whitcher.

***** /Counting biquadratic number fields that admit quaternionic or dihedral extensions/
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: 2025-12-12
:END:
      Louis Gaudet, UMass Amherst

Many interesting problems in arithmetic statistics involve counting
number fields (ordered by their discriminants, say) with certain
properties. In joint work with Siman Wong (UMass Amherst), we establish
asymptotic formulae for the number of biquadratic extensions of
\(\mathbb{Q}\) that admit a degree-2 extension with Galois group \(G\),
where \(G\) is either the quaternion group or the dihedral group (of
order 8). We will discuss these results and how they are proved, and we
will discuss their significance with regard to a theorem of Tate on
lifts of projective Galois representations.

***** /The distribution of 2-Selmer groups in quadratic twist families of elliptic curves/
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: 2026-02-27
:END:
      [[https://sites.google.com/view/ariel-weiss/][Ariel Weiss]], Trinity College

The Poonen–Rains and Bhargava–Kane–Lenstra–Poonen–Rains heuristics give
striking predictions for the distribution of Selmer groups in the family
of all elliptic curves over \(\mathbb{Q}\). In particular, they predict
that the average size of the \(p\)-Selmer group is \(1+p\), a result
proved for \(p = 2\), \(3\), and \(5\) by Bhargava and Shankar.

However, these models do not accurately describe families of elliptic
curves with isogenies, where the average \(p\)-Selmer size can even be
infinite. In this talk, I will report on work in progress to determine
the distribution of 2-Selmer groups in the family of quadratic twists of
an elliptic curve with a 2-torsion point. I will present a theorem that
shows that the distribution of the 2-Selmer groups coincides with a
distribution arising from the kernels of random matrices. This work is
joint with Harald Helfgott, Zev Klagsbrun, and Jennifer Park.

***** /The group theory of elliptic curves in towers of finite fields/
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: 2026-03-06
:END:
      [[https://faculty.bard.edu/cullinan/][John Cullinan]], Bard College

If \(E\) and \(E'\) are elliptic curves defined over a finite field
\(k\) such that \(E(k)\) and \(E'(k)\) have the same order, what is the
likelihood that they define isomorphic groups? In this talk we will
address this question from two points of view: fix \(E\), vary \(p\),
and fix \(p\), vary \(E\). We will not assume more than a working
knowledge of elliptic curves. This is recent and ongoing work with
Nathan Kaplan of UC Irvine.

***** /The Uniformization Theorem/
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: 2026-04-24
:END:
      [[https://mathematics.colostate.edu/person/?id=AAF7E91E00F35E3D1E382B1E38985927&sq=t][Will Allen]], Wesleyan University

The Uniformization Theorem establishes, up to appropriate notions of
equivalence, a one-to-one correspondence between lattices in the complex
plane and complex elliptic curves. In this talk, we give an overview of
the correspondence, and then explore in detail one of the properties of
this correspondence: that for any lattice \(\Lambda\) which is
associated to an elliptic curve \(E_\Lambda\), there is a bijection
\(\mathbb{C}/\Lambda \to E_\Lambda\) that gives an isomorphism of both
Riemann surfaces and abelian groups.

***** /On the cuspidal cohomology of congruence subgroups of \(\mathsf{SL}(3,\mathbb{Z})\)/
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: 2026-05-01
:END:
      [[https://zporat.github.io/][Zachary Porat]], Wesleyan University

In their 1984 work, Ash, Grayson, and Green compute the action of Hecke
operators on the cohomology of congruence subgroups \(\Gamma_0(3,p) \leq
\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{Z})\) for small \(p\). A natural extension is to
work with other congruence subgroups of \(\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{Z})\).
In the first part of the talk, we detail techniques for working with
congruence subgroups that are Iwahori at \(p\), computing the action of
Hecke operators on the corresponding cohomology. In the second part of
the talk, we return to the \(\Gamma_0(3,p)\) setting, introducing a new
technique which allows for computations directly on the cuspidal
cohomology.
